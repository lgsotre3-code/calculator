How Lenders Calculate Pre-Approval

Lenders test two debt-to-income (DTI) ratios. The front-end ratio caps your new housing payment — principal, interest, property tax, home insurance and HOA dues — at a percentage of your gross monthly income. The back-end ratio caps that same housing payment plus all your other monthly debts (car loans, student loans, credit cards) at a higher percentage. Whichever ratio produces the lower maximum payment is the binding constraint, and that payment is what determines your maximum loan amount and home price.

Conventional, FHA and VA Ratios Compared

Conventional loans typically use a 28% front-end and 36% back-end cap, though many lenders extend the back-end ratio to 45% for strong credit profiles. FHA loans are more flexible at 31% front-end and 43% back-end, which is why they often qualify buyers with higher existing debt. VA loans skip a strict front-end ratio and instead combine a roughly 41% back-end guideline with a residual-income test that verifies you have enough left over for living expenses after the mortgage.

Why PMI and Down Payment Size Matter

Putting down less than 20% on a conventional loan triggers private mortgage insurance (PMI), which adds to your monthly housing payment and reduces the loan amount you can qualify for under the same DTI caps. A larger down payment lowers your loan-to-value ratio, can remove PMI entirely, and frees up more of your DTI budget for principal and interest — which is often the fastest way to increase your pre-approval amount without raising your income.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do lenders calculate pre-approval amounts?

Lenders use two debt-to-income (DTI) ratios: the front-end ratio caps your housing payment (principal, interest, taxes, insurance and HOA) at a percentage of gross monthly income, and the back-end ratio caps your housing payment plus all other monthly debts at a higher percentage. Whichever ratio produces the lower maximum payment is the binding constraint, and that payment is used to back-solve your maximum loan amount.

What DTI ratios do conventional, FHA and VA loans use?

Conventional loans commonly use 28% front-end and 36% back-end. FHA loans allow more room at 31% front-end and 43% back-end. VA loans do not use a strict front-end cap but typically target around 41% back-end DTI combined with a residual-income test.

Is a pre-approval estimate the same as a lender's official pre-approval letter?

No. This tool gives an educational estimate using public DTI guidelines. An official pre-approval requires a lender to verify your income, assets, credit score and debts, and can differ from this estimate based on your credit profile, reserves and specific loan program.

Does my credit score change my pre-approval amount?

This calculator does not factor in credit score directly, but a lower score usually means a higher interest rate, which reduces the loan amount your budget supports. A higher score typically unlocks better rates and sometimes higher back-end DTI allowances.

How can I increase my pre-approval amount?

The three biggest levers are paying down existing monthly debts to free up back-end DTI room, increasing your down payment to reduce or eliminate PMI, and improving your credit score to qualify for a lower interest rate.
