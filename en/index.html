Should You Refinance? | Refinance Mortgage Calculator Guide

If you are considering a new loan, use a refinance mortgage calculator to compare your current terms with a new rate and term. The mortgage refinance break-even calculator principle tells you how many months it takes for the monthly savings to cover the closing costs — typically the break-even point is reached in 24 to 48 months.

Mortgage Refinance Break-Even Calculator

To find your break-even point, divide the total closing costs by the monthly savings from the new payment. For example, if refinancing saves $250 per month and costs $6,000 in fees, you break even in 24 months — after that, every month is pure savings.

Types of Mortgages: FHA, VA, USDA, Jumbo, and Conventional

Different loans suit different buyers. Government-backed programs like the FHA mortgage calculator, VA mortgage calculator, and USDA mortgage calculator typically offer lower down payments for qualifying borrowers, while conventional loans often reward strong credit with lower rates.

FHA, VA and USDA Loans

FHA loans allow down payments as low as 3.5% with a credit score around 580. VA loans offer zero down payment for eligible veterans. USDA loans target rural buyers. Each has its own insurance or guarantee fee — a mortgage insurance calculator can help you estimate the true monthly cost.

Jumbo and Conventional Loans

Jumbo mortgage calculators handle loan amounts above the conforming limit, which for 2026 is $766,550 in most U.S. counties. Conventional loans (fixed or adjustable rate) are the most common option and are what this calculator models by default.

Frequently Asked Questions About Mortgage Calculators

What is the formula for calculating a mortgage payment?

The monthly mortgage payment formula is M = P [ i(1 + i)^n ] / [ (1 + i)^n – 1 ], where M = monthly payment, P = principal, i = monthly interest rate, and n = total number of payments.

What is the principal and interest breakdown of a mortgage payment?

Each payment splits into principal (the amount that reduces your loan balance and builds equity) and interest (the cost of borrowing). Early in the loan most of the payment goes to interest; over time the split shifts until almost the entire payment goes to principal.

How do property taxes and insurance affect my monthly payment?

Lenders often collect them through an escrow account, adding a monthly portion to your payment. Property tax is usually a percentage of the home value, while home insurance is a fixed annual premium divided by 12. Including both gives you the true monthly cost — this is what makes a mortgage calculator with taxes and insurance different.

How do extra payments help pay off my mortgage faster?

Extra payments go directly to the principal, so you pay less interest on the remaining balance and shorten the loan term. For example, adding $100 per month to a $350,000 loan at 6.5% can save tens of thousands in interest and shave years off the payoff date.

How much mortgage can I afford based on my income?

A common guideline is the 28/36 rule: keep housing costs at or below 28% of gross income and total debt at or below 36%. A mortgage affordability calculator applies your exact income, debts, down payment and rate to find a realistic price range.

Should I make a bigger down payment?

A larger down payment means a smaller loan, lower monthly payments, and less total interest — and avoiding PMI when you put down 20% or more. The trade-off is tying up cash you might need elsewhere, so weigh a bigger down payment against your savings and investment goals.

Fixed-rate or adjustable-rate mortgage: which is better?

A fixed-rate mortgage keeps the same interest rate and payment for the whole term, which makes budgeting predictable. An adjustable-rate mortgage (ARM) starts lower but can rise after the initial period. If you plan to stay long-term, a fixed rate is usually safer.

What is PMI and when can I drop it?

Private mortgage insurance (PMI) protects the lender when your down payment is below 20%. A mortgage calculator with PMI shows the extra monthly cost. Once you reach 20% equity — either through payments or appreciation — you can request to remove PMI and lower your payment.

When does it make sense to refinance my mortgage?

Refinancing makes sense when you can lower your rate enough to recover closing costs within a reasonable time, or when you want to shorten the term. Use a refinance mortgage calculator to compare your current and new payments and find your break-even month.

What is an amortization schedule?

An amortization schedule is a table listing every payment over the life of the loan, showing how much goes to principal, how much to interest, and the remaining balance after each month. This mortgage amortization calculator generates it instantly and can include extra payments.

Do biweekly mortgage payments really save money?

Yes. Paying half your monthly payment every two weeks results in 26 half-payments a year — the equivalent of 13 full payments instead of 12. That extra payment per year goes to principal, cutting years off the loan and reducing total interest substantially.
